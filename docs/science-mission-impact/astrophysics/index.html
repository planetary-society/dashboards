Astrophysics

Astrophysics studies stars, galaxies, black holes, planets around other stars, and the early Universe. Nearly all its missions are space telescopes, flown above the atmosphere that blocks or blurs much of the light they collect. Some survey the whole sky; others point at chosen targets. Most are Explorers, usually competed and led by a principal investigator. A few are flagships, such as Hubble and Chandra, built around large, ultra-stable optics.

25 missions in this study, with 63,448 tracked publications and 3,422,497 citations to date.

Small and larger missions

Small missions, those that cost $150 million or less, make up 20% of Astrophysics’ missions in this study and 0.94% of its spending. They produced under 0.01% of its top-10% papers, the ones other scientists cited most. Larger missions also drew more citations per dollar.
