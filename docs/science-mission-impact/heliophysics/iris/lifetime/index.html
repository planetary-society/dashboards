Citation indices
h-indexh-index: the largest h such that h papers have at least h citations each. It only grows with time, so older missions score higher. Methods66
g-indexg-index: the largest g for which the g most-cited papers together hold at least g² citations. Like the h-index, but it lets the most-cited papers count for more. Methods99
m-indexm-index: a mission’s h-index divided by the years since its first peer-reviewed paper. It falls every Jan. 1, even when nothing else changes; the figure shown is as of Nov. 11, 2026. It also discounts the long operating life that larger missions paid for. Methods4.7
toritori (total research impact, from SciX): for every paper citing one of the mission’s papers, 1 divided by the citing paper’s reference count times the cited paper’s author count, summed, with self-citations removed. It favors citations from papers with short reference lists and from outside the mission’s own authors. Computed over the tracked citation graph, which can be slightly incomplete. Always over every tracked publication to date, whichever scope is chosen. Methods63
riqriq (research impact quotient): 1,000 times the square root of tori, divided by the years since the mission’s first paper. A rate, not a total, so it does not keep growing with age the way the h-index and tori do. Like tori, always over every tracked publication to date. Methods568

How we found these papers

We searched NASA’s Science Explorer (SciX) for peer-reviewed articles naming IRIS in the title, abstract or keywords; standard filters drop articles that are not peer-reviewed and magazine pieces.
