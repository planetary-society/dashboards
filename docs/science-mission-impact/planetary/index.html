About this study

Tracked publications are the research papers we found for these missions; the list may not be complete. Citations are references to those papers by other papers—a measure of research attention, not a complete measure of scientific value.

Publication years: 1990–2026. Mission selection: Launch Date <= 2026-10-03 (launched missions only) AND Excluding hosted-payload missions AND Division = "Planetary Science" AND Launch Date > 1980-01-01 AND Publication window mature by 2026-10-03 AND Nation in {"United States*"} AND Mission Type not in {"Hosted Instrument"} AND Mission Type not in {"Instrument"} AND Short Title not in {"Clementine"}

About the comparison window

Publication windows follow each mission’s history. Papers need enough citation follow-up to enter these comparisons.

Active Mission Window: Mission-specific publication windows, from the month after science starts through the month 2 years after the mission ends (never shorter than 2 years), cut at the latest date that is mature on October 3, 2026. Exact exported boundaries are shown on each mission page. Citations count from the year a paper appeared through the third calendar year after it (4 calendar years in all). A paper naming several missions is split evenly among the missions in this division that claim it, so counts can be fractional. Measured zero, unavailable output, and mission failure are distinct. A missing publication source is not measured zero. Methods

How to read this chart

m-index: a mission’s h-index divided by the years since its first peer-reviewed paper. It falls every 1 January even when nothing else changes, so it belongs to the date shown; it also discounts the long operating life that larger missions paid for. h-index: the largest h such that h papers have at least h citations each. It only grows with time, so older missions score higher. Cost: life-cycle cost including partner contributions, in 2025 dollars (NASA’s New Start Index). Methods
