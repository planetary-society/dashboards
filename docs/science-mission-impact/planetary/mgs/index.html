Mars Global Surveyor

Orbiter · Directed · Agency-led · Launched on November 7, 1996 · Completed · LCC: $250M/$550M (2025 $)Life-cycle cost: NASA’s reported cost to build, launch, and operate a mission through its prime mission, plus documented partner contributions, in 2025 dollars. Extended missions are not included. Methods

Scope Papers published from the first full month after science operations begin through two years after the mission ends. Citations are counted through the third calendar year after each paper appears. For Mars Global Surveyor, papers published April 1, 1999 to December 1, 2008. Methods/ Every tracked publication to date, with every citation to date. For Mars Global Surveyor, papers published from 1997 to October 30, 2026. Methods
Tracked publicationsTracked publications: peer-reviewed papers we found that name the mission or its instruments in their titles, abstracts, or keywords. Papers that used the data without naming it are missed. Methods821821
CitationsCitations: how often other papers cited these publications. Each paper’s citations count over the year it appeared and the three years after, so older missions get no head start. Methods11,94211,942
Top-10% papersTop-10% papers: the 10% most-cited papers from this division’s missions, all publication years ranked together. They mark the results other scientists used most. A paper naming several missions is split evenly among the missions in this division that claim it, so counts can be fractional. Methods7979
Top-1% papersTop 1%: among the 1% most-cited papers from this division’s missions within the selected window, all publication years ranked together. A paper naming several missions is split evenly among the missions in this division that claim it, so counts can be fractional. Methods1010
Citation indices
h-indexh-index: the largest h such that h of the window’s papers have at least h citations each, counting each paper’s citations only over its citation window. Methods4343
g-indexg-index: the largest g for which the g most-cited papers together hold at least g² citations. Like the h-index, but it lets the most-cited papers count for more. Here, over the window’s papers and their windowed citations. Methods6565
m-indexm-index: the window’s h-index divided by the window’s length in years. It discounts the long operating life that larger missions paid for. Methods4.44.4
toritori (total research impact, from ADS): for every paper citing one of the mission’s papers, 1 divided by the citing paper’s reference count times the cited paper’s author count, summed, with self-citations removed. It favors citations from papers with short reference lists and from outside the mission’s own authors. Computed over the tracked citation graph, which can be slightly incomplete. Always over every tracked publication to date, whichever scope is chosen. Methods698698
riqriq (research impact quotient): 1,000 times the square root of tori, divided by the years since the mission’s first paper. A rate, not a total, so it does not keep growing with age the way the h-index and tori do. Like tori, always over every tracked publication to date. Methods880880

How we found these papers

We searched NASA’s Astrophysics Data System (ADS) for peer-reviewed articles naming Mars Global Surveyor in the title, abstract or keywords; standard filters drop articles that are not peer-reviewed and magazine pieces. SciX is ADS’s current interface.
